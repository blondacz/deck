## Context

The pergola footprint is 5800 mm x 3100 mm and sits next to French doors, with a Strada Carbo-style polycarbonate roof, RAL 7016 frame, and three anthracite aluminium legs on the garden side. The pergola documentation lists a 2660 mm pass-through height, three 3000 mm posts, 120 mm x 120 mm posts, and foundation pads below zero level. Two of the garden-side legs contain gutter drain outlets that discharge away from the pergola, and the legs are already attached to concrete.

On the house side, the turf is about 200 mm below the French door threshold. Existing concrete pads are about 100 mm below the threshold, and the soil is sandy and easy to excavate. The ground below the planned deck is sloped and must be stabilized before installing a long-lasting timber surface.

The deck should be slightly larger than the pergola footprint so the walking surface feels integrated with the house entrance and does not terminate awkwardly at the pergola line. The design must fit Czech exterior conditions, including rain, snow, freeze-thaw movement, and common locally available materials.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- Create a durable, level wooden deck surface under and around the pergola.
- Use a foundation build-up of compacted ground, geotextile, gravel, concrete garden tiles, and adjustable plastic supports.
- Keep the timber structure separated from soil and standing water.
- Preserve pergola leg fixings and keep gutter drain outlets free-flowing and serviceable.
- Define a procurement-friendly approach using materials available from Czech suppliers such as Artisan, Bauhaus, DEK, or equivalents.

**Non-Goals:**

- Move or redesign the pergola, roof, guttering, or aluminium legs.
- Design a fully enclosed or insulated floor assembly.
- Provide structural engineering certification or substitute for local code review where required.
- Select a single mandatory supplier or product SKU.

## Decisions

1. Use either adjustable plastic pedestal supports or fixed frame spacer pads on concrete bearing elements.

Adjustable supports allow precise leveling over the sloped base without embedding timber into wet ground. Fixed frame spacer pads are also acceptable where the height is already correct and only a separation layer is needed under the frame. Concrete garden tiles or flat-laid garden edging blocks (`zahradni obrubnik`) spread point loads and provide replaceable, stable bearing points. Alternatives considered were direct timber posts in concrete footings and a fully poured concrete slab; posts increase moisture risk and slab work is more invasive, expensive, and harder to reverse.

The DITON concrete grass paver from Bauhaus (`zatravnovaci dlazdice`, 600 x 400 x 80 mm) is suitable as a hidden perimeter bearing and edge-retaining element under the deck perimeter, especially to keep turf and sand from migrating into the gravel and to provide a base for edge studs or fascia support fixings. It should not replace the flat-laid edging blocks or concrete tiles used for the main internal support bearing points unless its void pattern, bearing area, and fixing method are checked for the chosen support hardware.

Supports or spacer pads should be mechanically fixed to the concrete tile or flat-laid edging block where the product design allows it and where fixing does not crack the concrete bearing element. The fixing method must match the support manufacturer's guidance, screw type, plug type if needed, and minimum edge distances. Edge grass pavers may support hidden edge studs or fascia support points, but screw anchoring must land in a solid web section with safe edge distance and pull-out resistance.

2. Use geotextile and compacted gravel below the tiles.

Geotextile separates soil from aggregate and limits weed growth, while gravel improves drainage and creates a stable bedding layer. Alternatives considered were only garden tiles on soil or plastic weed membrane only; both are more likely to settle, trap moisture, or allow vegetation pressure over time.

3. Keep drainage paths independent of the deck frame.

The two pergola leg drain outlets must discharge outside the pergola without being blocked by joists, fascia boards, gravel edges, or decking boards. If the outlet path crosses the deck perimeter, the design must leave a visible service gap or route water into a protected gravel splash zone. An alternative was hiding drainage behind fascia, but that makes clogging and water damage harder to detect.

4. Size the deck slightly beyond the pergola footprint while maintaining perimeter ventilation.

The deck should extend beyond 5800 mm x 3100 mm enough to create a clean transition from the French doors and cover the useful walking zone, but not so far that unsupported edges, trapped leaves, or difficult maintenance become likely. Final overhang should be confirmed on site against door threshold height, pergola leg positions, facade details, and drain outlets.

The current height facts suggest the house-side build-up can fit a typical ventilated timber deck assembly if the finished deck stays below the door threshold and water is kept away from the facade. A separate threshold transition board or ledger-like board may be useful, but it must be detailed as a replaceable transition piece with drainage and ventilation gaps rather than a water-trapping sealed connection to the house.

5. Prefer corrosion-resistant and exterior-rated materials.

Fasteners, joist hangers if used, support heads, and timber treatment must suit exterior use in Czech climate. Stainless or approved coated fasteners reduce staining and failure risk. Untreated interior timber and non-UV-stable plastics are rejected.

Deck board spacing should be controlled with spacer pads or a spacing tool rather than estimated by eye. The `distancni podlozka` examples are useful for maintaining repeatable board gaps and reducing direct trapped contact between components.

The deck should be closed on three exposed sides with fascia boards. Prefer purpose-made smooth fascia boards if available for the selected timber species; otherwise use compatible non-grooved boards so the vertical face is easier to clean and looks intentional. Grooved terrace boards can be used only if no better matching option exists, but grooves on a vertical fascia are not preferred.

6. Treat the Bauhaus photos as reference examples, not final product selection.

The photos in `materials/examples/bgauhause_photos/` show candidate categories that fit the project: Bangkirai boards with stated high durability, impregnated pine/teak-tone boards, Douglas/larch options, European larch premium boards around 3000 x 140 x 32 mm, small plastic spacer pads around 0.5 x 4 x 8 cm with listed use around 14-16 pcs/m2, and SPAX terrace screws including A2 stainless variants. These examples should guide the procurement matrix, but final selection must still be checked against actual dimensions, warranty, stock, fastener compatibility, and total installed cost.

7. Excavate sandy soil only as much as needed for a compacted, draining base.

Because the soil is sandy and easy to dig, excavation should be used to correct slope and create consistent build-up depth, not to make unnecessary deep trenches. The foundation should maintain drainage away from the house, protect the existing pergola pads, and avoid undermining concrete footings.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- Ground settlement -> Compact the subgrade and gravel in layers, use enough tile support points, and recheck pedestal levels before fixing boards.
- Blocked pergola drainage -> Keep outlets visible or serviceable and test water flow before closing the deck perimeter.
- Moisture trapped under timber -> Maintain air gaps below joists and between boards, avoid direct timber-soil contact, and slope surrounding ground away from the house.
- Threshold mismatch at French doors -> Measure finished floor height before procurement and adjust support heights or board thickness accordingly.
- Water trapped at the French door connection -> Use a drained threshold transition detail with an inspection gap instead of sealing deck boards tightly to the facade.
- Existing concrete pads are disturbed -> Keep excavation away from pad edges unless their depth and stability are confirmed.
- Frost movement -> Use a draining gravel layer, avoid water pockets under tiles, and allow adjustable supports to be re-leveled if seasonal movement occurs.
- Product availability changes -> Specify performance requirements rather than relying on one exact supplier SKU.
- Photo labels may be incomplete or outdated -> Verify every candidate product on the supplier website or in-store label before purchase.
