## Deck Footprint Recommendation

Use a perfect rectangle that is larger than the pergola on two exposed sides: one long side and one short side. Keep the house side aligned to the French-door transition and do not enlarge toward the facade unless needed for the threshold detail.

Recommended enlargement: one full deck-board width on each of the two exposed sides.

For a common 140 mm board width, this gives an approximate finished deck footprint of:

```text
5800 mm + 140 mm = 5940 mm
3100 mm + 140 mm = 3240 mm
```

If the selected board is 145 mm wide, use approximately 5945 mm x 3245 mm. If the selected board is 120 mm wide, use approximately 5920 mm x 3220 mm.

One board width is the preferred default because it looks intentional, keeps the rectangle slightly larger without wasting much material, and avoids creating a wide unsupported perimeter. Use slightly more than one board width only if the final board layout would otherwise leave an awkward narrow rip cut, or if you want a more generous visible border around furniture.

If using a picture-frame border, set the enlargement from the actual border board width plus desired movement gap rather than from the nominal pergola dimension alone.

## French Door Transition Detail

Use a separate replaceable timber transition board or narrow deck-board strip at the French doors if needed to make the step comfortable.

Requirements for the detail:

- Keep the finished walking surface slightly below the threshold rather than flush above it.
- Do not seal timber tightly against the facade or threshold.
- Leave a small inspection and cleaning gap at the house side.
- Make the transition piece removable so dirt can be cleaned and the detail can be adjusted later.
- Keep any screws clear of waterproofing-sensitive facade or threshold parts unless the fixing substrate is confirmed.

## Material Choices Established So Far

- Board spacing: use `distancni podlozka` or an equivalent spacing tool, not visual spacing by eye.
- Perimeter support: DITON `zatravnovaci dlazdice` 600 x 400 x 80 mm replace obrubníks on all three exposed edges. Set them about 300 mm under the deck and 100 mm outside, or fully under where site obstacles require it. Edge joists bear on solid paver ribs through rubber pads.
- Long outside perimeter: do not force a continuous row through concrete piers. Existing piers replace pavers where they occupy the edge row; cut/field-fit pavers in gaps. Do not mechanically tie the deck to pergola leg piers unless necessary and clear of pergola anchors.
- Internal support bearing: use three rows of flat-laid `zahradni obrubnik` segments. Pavers carry the three exposed structural edges.
- Support type: use adjustable plastic supports where height varies; fixed frame spacer pads are acceptable where height is already correct.
- Drain pockets: under each pergola leg drain outlet, use a simple pebble/gravel soakaway pocket in the sandy soil rather than complex drainage pipework unless on-site water testing shows a problem.
- Fascia: cover the three exposed deck sides with purpose-made smooth fascia boards if available, or compatible non-grooved boards. Avoid grooved terrace boards for the vertical face unless no matching smooth option is available.
- Internal bearing blocks: each row uses six support segments from six DITON straight edging blocks (`1000 x 200 x 50 mm`). The left edge joist is carried by structural pavers, so no extra short obrubník is needed there. Three rows use 18 blocks; buy 20. Use as bearing, not structural anchors.
- Framing: use 16 full-depth joist lines. Butt joints use paired joists so each board end has independent support. Buy 17 joists.
- Joist restraint: use 2 small L brackets per internal obrubník segment, 36 exact/40 purchased. One concrete fixing and two wood screws per bracket. Do not drill grass pavers. Brackets restrain position only; concrete elements and gravel carry vertical load.

## Long Outside Edge With Concrete Piers

Before setting the long outside perimeter, mark the exact outline of all five concrete piers. Treat them as follows:

- Keep the deck rectangle and fascia line straight; adjust the hidden support layout below it rather than changing the visible deck edge.
- Skip grass pavers where a concrete pier already occupies the perimeter zone.
- Use stable existing concrete piers as bearing locations only after checking they are sound, level enough, and do not conflict with pergola anchors.
- Fill remaining gaps between piers with grass pavers or short concrete bearing pieces as needed.
- Turn geotextile up around the outside/soil side of both grass pavers and concrete piers so soil does not migrate into the gravel.
- Keep pebble pockets clear below the two drain legs even if they land near concrete piers.

## Maintenance Checklist

- Each spring and autumn, clear leaves, sand, and debris from deck gaps and perimeter edges.
- Inspect the two pergola drain outlets and pebble pockets for blockage.
- Check whether any board ends, screws, or transition pieces have moved or loosened.
- Check that timber remains clear of soil and gravel fines.
- Re-level adjustable supports if seasonal settlement creates rocking or uneven boards.
- Refresh timber finish according to the selected board manufacturer's guidance.
- Keep access points to drains and representative supports removable or inspectable.
